What it is

A small cattle-records system built to help visualize and manage herd changes. Logs are tracked using a spreadsheet and uploaded on a webpage.

The upload page can also build the spreadsheet for you. Say what happened and when (a vaccination, a calving, a purchase) and you get a template with the rows already started, so you only type what is new.

This data is simulated using a Python script: about 200 head and five years of simulated events.

Built to run for free

A lot of the decisions in this project came back to cost. The only thing I pay for is the domain name, about $11 a year. The database, the hosting, the upload function, the bot check, and the automation all run on free plans. The design had to fit inside their limits: a static site instead of a server, a rebuild budget, a cap on uploads. The details are on the next page: How it was built →

Current Reports

  • Herd Overview. How many head, by breed, location and birth year, and what calves weighed before they were sold.
  • Pregnancy & Calving. Which cows are expected to calve and when, and which calf belongs to which cow.
  • Financial. Purchases and sales by year, and the money in and out.
  • Animal Lookup. Pick any tag and see that animal's whole history: where it came from, its calves, its weights, and its status.

How to use it

  1. Open the upload page and choose what happened: a vaccination, weighing, pregnancy check, sale or death among animals already on the ranch, a birth, or a purchase. Pick the date, then download the pre-filled template. A blank one is there too.
  2. Check the rows. Animals already on the ranch get a row each, with the tag, breed, type and location filled in. A birth gets a row for each pregnant cow, with her tag in the mother column and the calf's breed, location and birth year filled in. A purchase gets as many empty rows as you ask for, plus a Transactions row ready for the price. Delete the rows that do not apply and fill in what is left blank, such as a calf's tag or a weight. The required columns are the tag number, date, event type, location, breed and type, and the dropdowns in the template list the allowed values. A Herd tab lists the animals already in the demo for reference; it is not uploaded.
  3. Upload it. You can upload the .xlsx file, upload a CSV, or paste a link to a Google Sheet that is shared as "anyone with the link". Complete the quick "are you a person" check and press Upload.
  4. Read the answer. If a row has a problem, nothing is saved and the page lists every problem with its sheet, row and column, so you can fix it and try again. If everything passes, the whole file is saved at once, with a note for anything the server filled in for you, such as the tags on a purchase's Transactions row.
  5. See the result. The dashboards rebuild automatically; your rows usually show up within two or three minutes. Look up one of your tags on the Animal Lookup page.

Please use made-up data. It is a shared demo: files are limited to 1 MB and 500 rows per tab, each visitor can save five uploads an hour, and uploaded data is removed after seven days.
