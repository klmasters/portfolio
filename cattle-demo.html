What it is

A small cattle-records system built to show how a ranch's day-to-day data can become something useful. A ranch keeps a log of what happens to each animal: births, weigh-ins, vaccinations, pregnancy checks, purchases, sales. Here that log lives in a database, four dashboards read from it, and anyone can add to it by uploading a spreadsheet.

The herd is fake: about 200 head and five years of simulated events, generated by a script so the numbers look like a real operation without being one.

Built to run for free

Nearly every decision in this project came back to cost. The only thing I pay for is the domain name, about $11 a year. The database, the hosting, the upload function, the bot check and the automation all run on free plans, so the design had to fit inside their limits: a static site instead of a server, a rebuild budget, a cap on uploads. What's in it

  • Herd Overview. How many head, by breed, location and birth year, and what calves weighed before they were sold.
  • Pregnancy & Calving. Which cows are expected to calve and when, and which calf belongs to which cow.
  • Financial. Purchases and sales by year, and the money in and out.
  • Animal Lookup. Pick any tag and see that animal's whole history: where it came from, its calves, its weights, and its status.

How to use it

  1. Open the upload page and download the Excel template.
  2. Fill in a few rows. The Events tab takes one row per thing that happened to an animal. The columns you must fill are the tag number, the date, the event type (birth, purchase, weight, vaccination, pregnant, sold or deceased), the location, the breed and the type (cow, bull or calf). The dropdowns in the template list the allowed values. The Transactions tab is optional and is for purchases and sales with money attached. The Herd tab is just a list of the animals already in the demo, so you can reuse their tags.
  3. Upload it. You can upload the .xlsx file, upload a CSV, or paste a link to a Google Sheet that is shared as "anyone with the link". Complete the quick "are you a person" check and press Upload.
  4. Read the answer. If a row has a problem, nothing is saved and the page lists every problem with its sheet, row and column, so you can fix it and try again. If everything passes, the whole file is saved at once.
  5. See the result. The dashboards rebuild automatically; your rows usually show up within two or three minutes. Look up one of your tags on the Animal Lookup page.

Please use made-up data. It is a shared demo: files are limited to 1 MB and 500 rows per tab, each visitor can save five uploads an hour, and uploaded data is removed after seven days.

Where it stands

Working today

  • the four dashboards, reviewed page by page,
  • uploads from Excel, CSV and Google Sheets links,
  • checks on every row before anything is saved,
  • a bot check, a rate limit and a daily clean-up of old uploads,
  • automatic rebuilds after an upload.

Rough edges

  • The template's Herd tab is a snapshot, so animals added by uploads are not in it until I regenerate it.
  • Uploading from a Google Sheet link works but is the least tested path.
  • Bulls have only one event each in the simulated data, so their records are thin.

Up next

  • A template that is built fresh on each download, so its Herd list always includes uploaded animals.
  • "Last updated" and "data through" dates on every dashboard, and small data-quality counts (for example, how many animals have no weight on record).
  • Richer simulated data for bulls.
