What it is

A pipeline and a set of dashboards for one question: what is going on in the Kansas cattle market, and how do hay prices and drought fit in? Every week, scripts pull the latest public reports from USDA Market News (auction sales, direct sales of cattle, and hay) and from the U.S. Drought Monitor, clean them up and store them in a Postgres database. A few hours later the dashboards rebuild with the new week's numbers.

How it was built

The short version: scheduled scripts load four public reports into Postgres every Monday, a rebuild turns the database into a static dashboard site every Tuesday, and the site costs almost nothing to run. What's in it

  • Overview. The latest week at a glance: the 500-600 lb steer price against the week before, the alfalfa hay price against the last report, the share of Kansas in drought against a year ago, and direct cattle sales against auction.
  • Market Trends. Auction prices and volume over time. A weight class dropdown (400 to 800 lb) drives the latest-week numbers, the seasonal pattern and the heifer-discount chart.
  • Drought & Market Conditions. Kansas drought since 2000, and how it lines up with hay prices, steer prices and auction volume since 2020.
  • Backgrounding Economics. What a lighter steer is worth against a heavier one, the "value of gain" between them, and what is left after paying for hay. A slider sets how many pounds of hay it takes to put on a pound of gain.
  • Direct vs. Auction. How cattle sold directly compare with the same cattle at the sale barn, by class and weight, and how much of the volume goes each way.

How to use it

  1. Open the dashboards and start with the Overview for the current week.
  2. Pick a weight class or class where a page has a dropdown. The numbers and charts on that page follow it.
  3. Narrow the dates. Charts with a date filter let you jump to the last year, the last three months, or any range.
  4. Try the slider on Backgrounding Economics to see how the answer changes as the hay assumption does. It is an assumption of mine, not USDA data.
  5. Hover over a chart to see exact values. On the comparison panels the hover lines up across charts.
